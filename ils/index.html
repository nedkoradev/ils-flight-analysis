How ILS works

Two independent guidance components.

ILS provides lateral and vertical guidance through two independent ground-based signal components. The airborne ILS receiver evaluates the modulation relationship of 90 Hz and 150 Hz components to determine deviation—not simply to “follow a radio beam.”

LOCALIZER (LOC)Lateral guidance relative to the extended runway centreline.
GLIDE PATH (GP)Vertical guidance relative to the nominal descent path.

The guidance principle

Modulation comparison produces guidance.

Difference in Depth of Modulation (DDM) is the principal guidance quantity. At the nominal course or path, the relevant 90 Hz and 150 Hz modulation depths are equal, so DDM is nominally zero. Away from it, one modulation component predominates.

ILS approach categories

Operational capability and minima.

ILS categories are not simply “accuracy levels.” They relate to operational capability and minima and depend on the complete system: ground facilities, airborne equipment, procedures, monitoring and other operational requirements. This is an introductory overview; detailed requirements should be taken from applicable authoritative aviation standards.

CAT I

An operational category requiring a defined combination of approved facilities, aircraft capability and procedures.

CAT II

A lower-minima operational capability with additional system, monitoring and procedural requirements.

CAT III

Operations at the lowest minima, subdivided by applicable standards and dependent on the complete operational system.
